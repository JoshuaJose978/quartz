# Full Version
## Introduction

In this video, I'm going to show you how to make $1 million profit from design. I'll start with mindset and theory - the framework for how to think to make this happen. Then I'll share the most important strategic and financial ideas I've discovered in my 40 years in the industry.

I'll give you tangible examples of:

- How to get paid for ideas
- Vertical integration
- The importance of skill stacking
- Technology and narrative
- How to multiply your design fees in an unexpected way

If you pay attention, these ideas can change your life.

## Part 1: Mindset and Philosophy

### Rejecting the False Binary

In arts and academia, discussing money is often seen as an intrusion of commerce into creative integrity. I reject this false binary. In my 25 years as head of a graduate program at an art school, I've never seen an artist or designer turn down a museum sale.

Financial strategy isn't a betrayal of values - it's the infrastructure that allows work to exist. You don't future-proof a career by ignoring economics. You design systems that are sustainable, abundant, generative, and ethical - where you profit without exploiting others.

## Part 2: How Designers Build Wealth

### 1. Get Paid for Ideas, Not Labor

If your design career can be undone by a teenager with Midjourney and a Fiverr account, you have the wrong strategy.

**The Conceptual Hierarchy:**

- The lower you are in the conceptual hierarchy, the more at risk you are from AI and automation
- AI is already targeting execution, repetitive tasks, and "can you make it pop" work
- Manual labor (even digital) is low leverage
- Robots don't tire, take breaks, or need wellness days

**Solution:** Position yourself as high as possible in the conceptual stack. Get paid for your ideas. Become the author of the framework itself.

**Steps:**

1. Become aware of the value of ideas over labor
2. Work to position yourself highly in the conceptual stack

### 2. Vertical Integration

Own as much of the production stack as possible.

**Case Study Example:** A designer I know builds prototypes and owns the manufacturing chain:

- Owns the 3D printer for prototyping (cost: ~$40 in filament)
- Sets up the entire manufacturing process
- Material costs are 20% of total cost
- Only announces products after the entire production pipeline is ready
- Can fulfill orders within 7 days
- Doesn't manufacture until orders are confirmed

This approach is:

- Extremely low risk
- High control
- Tight margins
- No outside vendors, waiting, or markup leakage

**Traditional vs. Vertical Integration:**

- Traditional: Outsource everything, reduce margins, extend timelines, add uncertainty
- Vertical: Own the process, test the market, minimize upfront cash, maximize profits

### 3. Expand Your Skill Stack

Every successful designer I know never stops expanding their technical skills - not in a vague way, but skill by skill, relentlessly over decades.

**Your skill stack is:**

- How you survive in the age of AI
- Your independence plan
- Your career insurance policy

**Progressive Skill Building:**

- Start with fundamentals (product design)
- Add complexity (UX, 3D modeling, CNC, laser cutting)
- Learn prototyping (foam core, paper, resin, clay)
- Add casting (lost wax, investment casting)
- Layer in motion design, computational work
- Add code, sensors, shipping logistics

**Goal:** Reach a point where ideas aren't bottlenecked by lack of skills. Your imagination becomes the only limit, not your tooling.

### 4. No Fixed Definition of Your Discipline

Don't say "I'm a book designer" or "I'm a graphic designer." The moment you define yourself by a single medium, you limit yourself.

**Think of yourself as a cultural producer** - creating work that shapes meaning across whatever platform is most alive at the moment.

**Examples:** Virgil Abloh, Rick Owens, Björk, Tyler the Creator - they float between disciplines, chasing ideas rather than formats.

### 5. Stay on the Leading Edge of Technology

This is non-negotiable for future-proofing your career. Don't just be aware of technology - use it.

Designers who thrive treat tech as a collaborator, not a threat. You don't need to become a coder, but you must understand the tools changing your field.

### 6. Construct the Narrative Around Your Work

In a climate flooded with content and AI output, the work alone isn't enough. The audience needs context.

**People remember:**

- The framework
- Your voice
- Your point of view
- Your stance on culture

**Key insight:** People don't buy what you do, they buy why you do it. If you don't articulate the "why," you're just noise in the feed.

## Part 3: The Practical Method - Making $1 Million

### Core Principle: Long-term Goals Over Short-term Gratification

**Formula:** Discipline + Time = Freedom

The most powerful tool is compound interest - what Einstein called "the most powerful force in the universe."

### Live Below Your Means

- Making $40,000? Live on $25,000
- Making $150,000? Build your life around $100,000
- Invest the margin (don't just save it)

### Saving vs. Investing

- **Saving:** Protects money (~2.125% interest, barely beats inflation)
- **Investing:** Multiplies money through long-term, diversified, boring compounding

**Required Reading:** "The Intelligent Investor" by Benjamin Graham (Warren Buffett's mentor, published 1949, considered the Bible of value investing)

### The Million-Dollar Strategy: Multiply Design Fees Through Investment

**The Key Insight:** If you have the discipline to invest a portion of every client payment and never touch it, you can multiply your income without doing more work.

**Example Scenario:**

- $30,000 design fee
- Invest $10,000 (never touch it)
- At 7% annual return over 30 years: $10,000 becomes $66,123
- Your $30,000 fee effectively becomes $96,124
- The client unknowingly paid you $96,000 for that design work

**This scales down:** The same principle works with $1,000 or $100 design fees.

### Understanding Compound Interest

**Hypothetical Comparison (30 years, 7% annual return):**

- Investor A: Withdraws interest yearly = $21,000 total earnings
- Investor B: Reinvests interest = $66,123 total earnings
- Compound interest earned triple the returns

### Implementation

1. Get "The Intelligent Investor"
2. Learn value-based investing (not crypto, meme stocks, or gambling)
3. Invest a portion of every single design fee
4. Focus on defensive investing with strong fundamentals
5. Never touch the invested money

## Bonus Idea: Luxury Items Are Receipts, Not Assets

**Key insight:** Luxury goods are certificates of extraction - they prove you _had_ money, not that you _have_ money.

**Assets vs. Receipts:**

- Assets increase net worth
- Luxury goods decrease net worth
- A $3,000 bag is a receipt, not an asset

### Real Financial Power

- Isn't loud - it's quiet
- Accumulates rather than leaks
- Doesn't perform wealth - it builds it

### What to Buy Instead

1. **Tools that increase capability:** 3D printers, CNC machines, equipment that expands your skill stack
2. **Real assets:** Real estate, companies, investments that compound
3. **Your time and freedom**

Don't try to look rich - aim to be free. Let your future be the flex, and freedom be the outcome.
